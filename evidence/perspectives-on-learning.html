However, understanding the different theories of learning and considering which of them most closely aligns with your own views can better inform your teaching. The You Might Be a Constructivist If Inventory is an informal assessment intended to help you identify your own views of learning and ascertain where they fall within the primary existing theoretical frameworks. Clarifying your theoretical stance can help you express your views to others and best direct your teaching efforts going forward.

Scores Behaviorism: 0 You may find yourself selecting pedagogical activities that prioritize observable behavior, external rewards or punishments, and repetition to reinforce learning. This theory posits that human behavior can be explained in terms of conditioning, without appeal to thoughts or feelings, and that psychological disorders are best treated by altering behavior patterns. Cognitivism: 4 You may choose pedagogical activities that focus on mental processes involved in learning, such as information processing, memory, and cognitive load. This theory emphasizes the processes involved in learning rather than on the observed behavior. Constructivism: 2 You may select pedagogical activities that prioritize learners actively constructing knowledge through their experiences, reflection, and collaboration. This theory asserts that learners construct knowledge rather than just passively take in information. Humanism: 1 You may prioritize learners as individuals and select activities that emphasize their subjective experiences, autonomy, and personal growth. This theory emphasizes the value and agency of human beings, individually and collectively.
