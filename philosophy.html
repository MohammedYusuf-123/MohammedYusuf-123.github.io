TEACHING PHILOSOPHY

My teaching philosophy:
interest and understanding.

This section explains the beliefs I described in my AHE 603 teaching philosophy and how they relate to the activities in this portfolio.

THE CENTRAL BELIEF

Explain why the topic matters.
Encourage students to ask questions.

Read the original statement ↗

Developing interest in the subject

I believe students learn best when they are interested in the subject and understand why it is important. For example, I became interested in Java because I enjoyed playing games on mobile phones. This connection gave me a reason to understand how applications are developed. Similarly, I would use practical applications to introduce a topic so that students can relate the concept to something they already know.

Connecting concepts to familiar examples

As an educator, my role is to explain the concepts clearly and help students understand their practical use. A familiar example can provide a starting point before introducing more detailed information. In my original statement, I discuss using cars to explain combustion engines and wind energy to explain electricity generation. These examples show how an everyday application can be connected to a concept that may initially seem unfamiliar.

Encouraging questions and discussion

I believe that students should be able to ask questions without being afraid of making a mistake. When they explain their reasoning or ask why a process works, I can better understand how they are thinking about the topic. Even a small question can identify a misunderstanding that other students may also have. Therefore, I would encourage discussion and use students’ responses to decide whether further explanation is required.

Improving my teaching through feedback

I would continue to develop my teaching by examining which explanations students understand and which parts require additional support. My PCK response discusses dividing complex concepts into smaller parts and modifying teaching based on feedback. This approach can help me consider the pace of a lesson and the amount of information introduced at one time.

A CONNECTION DEVELOPED IN THE PORTFOLIO

Interest and participation are useful, but they do not provide complete evidence of understanding. My demonstration on Maya’s learning examines this limitation. A student may perform well on a familiar test question and still have difficulty applying the concept elsewhere. Therefore, I would also include tasks that ask students to explain and apply what they have learned.
