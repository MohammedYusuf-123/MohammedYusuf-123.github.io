Reading a Syllabus as a Learner

This activity reviews how a syllabus communicates course expectations, assessment requirements, and information students need to prepare.

Purpose of the activity

A syllabus provides information about a course, but it also communicates what the instructor values and what students are expected to do. This review examines the learning goals, grading approach, and practical information in a syllabus from the perspective of a student.

02 / REFLECTION ON THE ACTIVITY

Reflection

The review shows that students may have questions even when a syllabus includes the main administrative sections. For example, they may know the grading weights but still be unsure about the topics covered in an assignment or the preparation required. In my response, I also identified that there was not enough information to judge how well the assessments matched the outcomes. Without the assessment tasks, I cannot determine whether they measure the stated outcomes. I would need to review those tasks before judging their alignment.

03 / PROPOSED TEACHING APPLICATION

Future teaching application

When developing a syllabus, I would explain the purpose of the main assignments and how they relate to the learning outcomes. I would also make prerequisites, preparation requirements, and late-work expectations easy to locate. Reviewing the document from a student’s perspective can help identify information that needs further explanation.

AREA FOR FURTHER DEVELOPMENT

I would support my evaluation with the information available in the syllabus and clearly identify where additional details are needed.

Connection to AHE 603

  • Consider how you might best meet the instructional needs of all students.
